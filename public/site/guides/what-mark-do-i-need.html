What mark do I need on the final exam?

Two numbers decide it: what you have already banked, and how much weight is left.

The arithmetic

An assessment mark is not worth its face value — it is worth its face value times its weight. A 75 on something worth 30% of the course puts 22.5 marks into your final total, not 75.

So the mark you need on what is left is:

needed = (target − banked) ÷ remaining weight × 100

where banked is the sum of every mark you already have times its weight, and remaining weight is everything you have not sat yet.

A worked example

A course with three pieces of assessment. You have sat two of them.

AssessmentWeightYour markBanked
Essay30%7522.5
Quiz20%6012.0
Exam50%——
Total so far50% sat34.5

You are carrying 34.5 marks into an exam worth 50. That makes the exam mark you need:

GradeNeeds a final mark ofSo the exam needs
Pass5030%
Credit6560%
Distinction7580%
High Distinction85100%

The Credit line is the one worth staring at. A 60 on the exam is a worse exam mark than either of the two you have already got, and it is enough for a Credit — because the two you have got are carrying it. That is the whole reason this is worth calculating rather than guessing: the number is almost never what it feels like.

The rounding rule changes the answer

Universities differ on how they turn a raw weighted mark into the one they record. Most round to the nearest whole number, so 74.5 becomes a 75. Some truncate, so 74.9 stays a 74.

That is not a technicality. On the same course above:

GradeRounded to nearestRounded down
Credit60%61%
Distinction80%81%
High Distinction100%impossible

A whole mark of difference on every band — and at the top it decides whether the grade is reachable at all. Under rounding-to-nearest an HD needs a perfect exam; rounded down it needs 101, which is to say it is gone whatever you do. Check your unit outline, or your university's assessment policy, and use the rule it states.

Three things that break the arithmetic

Hurdles. Plenty of courses require a minimum on the exam regardless of your total — commonly 40% or 50%. If a hurdle is higher than the mark the grade needs, the hurdle is the real answer. In the example above a 50% exam hurdle means a Pass needs 50, not 30.

Weights that do not add to 100. A unit outline totalling 90% or 105% is usually a typo and occasionally real. Do not scale it silently: the arithmetic on a wrong denominator produces a confident wrong answer, which is worse than no answer.

Scaling and moderation. Some faculties adjust marks after the fact. Nothing you can calculate in advance will predict that, and it is normally small.
