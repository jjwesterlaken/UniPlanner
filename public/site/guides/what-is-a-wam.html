What is a WAM?

Your Weighted Average Mark: the average of your unit marks, weighted by how big each unit was.

Weighted by credit points, not by unit count

A plain average treats every unit as equal. A WAM does not: a unit worth 12 credit points counts twice as much as one worth 6, because it was twice as much of your degree.

WAM = Σ (mark × credit points) ÷ Σ (credit points)

Marks, not grades. A WAM is built from the percentage on your transcript, which is why two students with identical grades can have WAMs several points apart.

A worked example

1842 ÷ 24 = 76.75.

The plain average of 78, 65 and 82 is 75. The WAM is nearly two marks higher, because the best mark was on the biggest unit. That gap is the entire point of weighting, and it runs both ways — a poor mark in a double-weighted unit costs twice as much as the same mark in a normal one.

A WAM is not a GPA

WAMGPA
Scale0–100, a percentageUsually 0–4 or 0–7, a points scale
Built fromYour actual marksYour grades, converted to points
SensitivityA 79 and an 84 differA 79 and an 84 are usually the same grade, so the same points

Because a GPA converts marks to grades first, it throws away where in the band you sat. That is why honours entry, scholarships and exchange places in Australia are frequently quoted as a WAM rather than a GPA — it is the finer measure.

Your university's formula may not be the obvious one

The definition above is the common one, and it is not universal. Things that genuinely vary between institutions, and sometimes between faculties:

Whether first-year units count. Some universities weight later years more heavily, or exclude first year from an honours calculation entirely.

What happens to a fail. Some formulas include the actual mark on a failed unit, some substitute a fixed value, some exclude it. This is the single biggest source of disagreement between a hand-calculated WAM and an official one.

Ungraded, credited and withdrawn units. Cross- institutional credit and pass/fail units usually have no mark to contribute, so they normally sit outside the calculation.

Which WAM. Several universities publish more than one — a course WAM, a career WAM, an honours WAM — computed over different sets of units for different purposes.

So a number you work out yourself is an estimate. Your university's own figure, in its results system or academic-progress page, is the one that decides anything, and its assessment policy is where the formula is written down.

What moves a WAM, and what doesn't

Later units move it less than they look like they should, because each new unit is a smaller share of a growing total. Ten equally sized units averaging 70, plus an eleventh at 85, gives 71.4 — a result fifteen marks above your average moves it by less than a mark and a half. That cuts both ways, so it is a reason not to panic about a single bad result, and a reason early units are worth more attention than they usually get.
